Every employer must insure its workers employed in Switzerland against accidents (art. 1a LAA, Federal Accident Insurance Act): with Suva if its activity is on the list in art. 66 LAA, otherwise with an insurer of its choice. In 2027, the maximum insured earnings stay at CHF 148,200 a year per person, an amount derived from the figures the Federal Council published on 26.08.2026.

## Must your business insure with Suva or can it choose?

Suva is compulsory for the sectors listed in art. 66 para. 1 LAA: industry, construction, installations, forestry operations, work on metal, wood, stone or glass with machines, transport, electricity, gas and water, temporary work agencies and the federal administration, among others. The Federal Council specifies this list (art. 66 para. 2 LAA).

Other businesses (trade, services, offices) choose an authorised private insurer, a health insurance fund or a public fund. A business subject to Suva cannot place its basic LAA cover with a private insurer.

## What are the LAA figures for 2027?

The maximum insured earnings stay at CHF 148,200 a year, an amount derived from the Federal Council’s official figures of 26.08.2026. For self-employed people, the access threshold for optional LAA falls from CHF 66,690 to CHF 44,460 on 01.01.2027. Net premiums for occupational and non-occupational accidents depend on each insurer’s tariff and on the business’s risk class.

## What can you compare?

Outside Suva, basic LAA premiums vary from one insurer to another. Supplementary LAA cover also makes a difference: depending on the contract, it covers salary above CHF 148,200, a private ward in hospital, benefits above 80% and capital on disability or death. A self-employed manager can choose between optional LAA (art. 4 and 5 LAA) and private accident insurance.

## What are the common pitfalls?

They mostly concern Suva affiliation, high salaries, part-time staff and the end of employment.

1. An activity that falls under Suva is insured with a private insurer, which does not match the law (art. 66 LAA).
2. Managers earn more than CHF 148,200 and the business has no supplementary cover, so the part of the salary above that amount is not covered.
3. Part-time staff working under 8 hours a week are not covered by the LAA for non-occupational accidents (art. 13 OLAA). Their health insurance covers them instead.
4. When employment ends, cover stops at the end of the 31st day after the end of the right to at least half the salary (art. 3 para. 2 LAA). The insurer must offer insurance by agreement, which extends cover by up to 6 months (art. 3 para. 3 LAA), and departing employees benefit from being told about it.

## How do you terminate LAA cover?

For basic LAA with a private insurer, the law sets neither a term nor an ordinary notice period. They come from the contract with the insurer (art. 59 para. 2 LAA), which is worth reading before a call for tenders. If the insurer raises the premium, the employer can terminate within 30 days of being notified, and the increase must be announced at least two months before the end of the financial year (art. 59a para. 2 LAA). Employees have the right to take part in choosing the insurer (art. 69 LAA). Accidents that happened before the change stay with the former insurer (art. 112 OLAA).

Supplementary LAA cover falls under the LCA (Insurance Contract Act). It can be terminated for the end of the 3rd year, then for the end of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA), or after a claim (art. 42 LCA).

For the employee side, the page [Accident insurance](/en/accident-insurance/) explains the 8-hour rule and the suspension of accident cover in the LAMal (compulsory basic health insurance).

## What this page does not cover
