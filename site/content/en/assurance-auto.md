In Switzerland, only motor vehicle liability insurance is compulsory, with a minimum of CHF 5 million per event (art. 3 OAV, Motor Vehicle Insurance Ordinance). Casco cover, partial or comprehensive, protects your own vehicle and is optional.

## What car insurance is compulsory in Switzerland?

No motor vehicle may be used on public roads without motor vehicle liability insurance (RC) (art. 63 para. 1 of the Road Traffic Act, LCR). This insurance pays for damage you cause to other people with your vehicle.

The minimum cover set by art. 3 OAV has not changed since 2005:

- CHF 5 million per event, personal injury and property damage combined;
- CHF 10 million for a vehicle with 10 to 50 seats;
- CHF 20 million above 50 seats.

Many contracts provide a much higher liability sum, often CHF 100 million.

## What does partial and comprehensive casco cover?

Partial casco covers theft, fire, natural hazards, glass breakage, damage caused by martens and collisions with animals. Comprehensive casco includes those risks and adds damage to your own vehicle in a collision.

## What sets two car insurance contracts apart?

Contracts differ first in the liability sum above the legal minimum and in the excesses, which are often higher for young drivers. It is also worth looking at:

- the bonus system and the bonus protection option;
- the exact list of events covered by partial casco;
- compensation after a total loss, at replacement value or at current value plus a surcharge;
- whether the insurer waives its right of recourse in case of gross negligence.

Comprehensive casco makes most sense for a vehicle that still has some value.

## What are the common mistakes?

1. Liability cover can exclude property damage suffered by the spouse or relatives living in the same household as the keeper (art. 63 para. 3 let. b LCR).
2. After an accident under the influence of alcohol or involving speeding, the insurer can claim money back from you, unless the contract waives recourse.
3. Comprehensive casco pays out less and less as the vehicle loses value. On an older car, the premium can exceed what you would receive.
4. If a person under 25 who is not named in the policy drives the car, an additional excess often applies.

## When can you cancel car insurance?

The contract can be cancelled for the end of the 3rd year, then of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a of the Insurance Contract Act, LCA). After a claim, both the insurer and the policyholder can end it (art. 42 LCA). You also have a 14-day right of withdrawal from the application or acceptance (art. 2a LCA).

When the vehicle is sold or changes keeper, the rights and obligations of the contract pass to the new keeper. The old contract lapses if the new registration certificate is issued with another insurer, and the old insurer can cancel within 14 days of learning of the change (art. 67 LCR). Handing in the plates at the cantonal office suspends the insurance (art. 68 para. 3 LCR).

If you are arriving in Switzerland with a vehicle, liability cover must be in place before you drive, and registration requires the insurance certificate. The [Moving to Switzerland](/en/moving-to-switzerland/) guide covers these steps.
