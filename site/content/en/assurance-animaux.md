No pet health insurance is compulsory in Switzerland. For dogs, however, Geneva, Vaud, Valais, Fribourg and Bern (Bernese Jura) require the owner to have liability insurance (RC).

Pet insurance reimburses part of the vet costs in case of illness or accident. Liability insurance covers damage your pet causes to others. Both are private-law contracts governed by the Insurance Contract Act (LCA).

## Is pet insurance compulsory in Switzerland?

No pet health insurance is compulsory. For dogs, Geneva, Vaud, Valais, Fribourg and the Bernese Jura require owner’s liability insurance (RC), while Neuchâtel and Jura have no such obligation.

| Canton | Compulsory RC for the dog | Minimum sum | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geneva | Yes | Not set | Art. 16 LChiens |
| Vaud | Yes | Not set | Art. 15 LPolC |
| Valais | Yes, checked by the commune | Not set | Art. 31 LALPA |
| Fribourg | Yes | CHF 1 million per event | Art. 39 LDCh, art. 27 RDCh |
| Bernese Jura (Bern) | Yes | CHF 3 million | Art. 11 of the dog law, art. 29 of the ordinance |
| Neuchâtel | No obligation found | None | Dog law |
| Jura | No obligation found | None | No general dog law |

In Geneva and Fribourg, the dog’s control tag is issued only on presentation of an insurance certificate. Your personal liability insurance (RC privée) often covers the dog. Your contract says whether it does. In Fribourg and the Bernese Jura, where the law sets a minimum sum, the sum insured matters too.

## What sets two contracts apart?

The first difference is which vet costs are covered: illness, accident, or both. Contracts then differ on:

- the annual ceiling on reimbursement;
- the co-payment you pay;
- the maximum entry age of the animal;
- hereditary diseases, which are often excluded;
- the waiting period before cover starts;
- the dog’s liability cover, and whether your personal liability insurance already includes it.

## What are the common mistakes?

1. Premiums rise with age. A low premium for a puppy can become high for an older dog.
2. An illness known before the contract was concluded is generally not covered.
3. The ceiling per operation is sometimes low, even when the annual ceiling looks comfortable.
4. Separate liability insurance for a dog can duplicate your personal liability insurance (see [Household and personal liability insurance](/en/household-and-liability-insurance/)).

## What should you ask before signing?

- Does the contract cover illness, or only accidents?
- What is the annual ceiling, and is there a ceiling per operation?
- What co-payment remains at your expense on each bill?
- Up to what age can the animal be insured, and does the premium change with age?
- Which hereditary diseases are excluded for this breed?
- How long is the waiting period?
- For a dog, does your personal liability insurance already cover the damage it causes?

There is no public data on pet insurance premiums, so offers have to be compared one by one, from the general terms and conditions.

## When can you cancel pet insurance?

The contract can be cancelled for the end of the 3rd year, then of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA). Cancellation is also possible after a claim under art. 42 LCA, and the right of withdrawal lasts 14 days from the application or acceptance (art. 2a LCA).

## What this page does not cover

This page compares no premiums, for lack of public data, and does not cover municipal dog regulations. For the obligations, the authoritative source is the law of your canton. For the contract, it is the [LCA on Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/24/719_735_717/fr) and your general terms and conditions.
