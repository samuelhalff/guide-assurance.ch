If you are employed in Switzerland, your employer must insure you against accidents under the LAA (Federal Accident Insurance Act, art. 1a LAA). Without the LAA, your basic health insurance (LAMal, compulsory basic health insurance) covers accidents, with the deductible and co-payment. Knowing which one applies keeps you from paying twice, or from being poorly covered at the end of a job.

## Who is covered by the LAA?

Employees working in Switzerland are insured compulsorily by their employer (art. 1a para. 1 let. a LAA). Non-occupational accidents, meaning those outside work, are covered only if you work at least 8 hours a week for one employer (art. 13 para. 1 OLAA, the Accident Insurance Ordinance). Below 8 hours a week, only the commute between home and work counts as an occupational accident (art. 13 para. 2 OLAA).

## Who is covered against accidents by the LAMal?

People without LAA cover are insured against accidents by their basic health insurance, with deductible and co-payment. This applies to children, students, retirees, self-employed people who are not insured under the LAA, and people who work fewer than 8 hours a week.

## What are the 2027 accident insurance figures?

Maximum insured earnings under the LAA stay at CHF 148,200 a year. The amount is derived from the thresholds the Federal Council published on 26.08.2026, where CHF 66,690 is 45% and CHF 44,460 is 30%.

For self-employed people, the access threshold for voluntary LAA falls from CHF 66,690 to CHF 44,460 (30% of maximum earnings) on 01.01.2027. The Federal Council took that decision on 26.08.2026.

## What should you compare in your situation?

If the LAMal covers you for accidents, the comparison is about the accident supplement in your premium. Employees will look instead at the supplementary LAA cover offered by the employer, which pays for example for the private hospital ward or insures the part of salary above CHF 148,200. Self-employed people choose between voluntary LAA and private accident insurance, whose benefits differ.

## What are the common mistakes?

1. An employee fully covered by the LAA pays for accidents twice if the LAMal premium still includes that risk. The health insurer suspends accident cover on request and on proof, and the premium goes down (art. 8 para. 1 LAMal).
2. At the end of a job, or when you drop below 8 hours a week, LAMal accident cover resumes as soon as the LAA ends (art. 8 para. 2 LAMal). Your health insurer needs to know so it can adjust the premium.
3. The end date of the LAA is easy to get wrong. Cover ends at the end of the 31st day after the right to at least half pay ends (art. 3 para. 2 LAA).
4. Insurance by agreement is easy to overlook. It extends the LAA by up to 6 months, the insurer must offer it to you (art. 3 para. 3 LAA), and it can bridge the gap between two jobs.

## How do you cancel or change accident cover?

LAA cover follows the employment contract, so you do not cancel it yourself (art. 3 LAA). Suspending or reactivating accident cover in the LAMal is a request to your health insurer (art. 8 LAMal). Private accident insurance and supplementary LAA cover fall under the LCA (Insurance Contract Act). They can be cancelled for the end of the 3rd year, then of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA).
