Without insurance, an employer must keep paying a sick employee’s salary for 3 weeks in the 1st year of service, then for a longer period set fairly (art. 324a para. 1 and 2 CO). Daily sickness benefits insurance (IJM) can take over this duty if its benefits are at least equivalent.

## What must the employer pay without insurance?

The employer pays the salary for 3 weeks in the 1st year of service, then for a “longer period set fairly” (art. 324a para. 1 and 2 CO), provided the contract has lasted more than 3 months or was concluded for more than 3 months.

Because the law does not put a figure on this period, courts use scales. The table published by the courts of the canton of Zurich gives the first three years:

| Year of service | Bernese scale | Basel scale | Zurich scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 3 weeks | 3 weeks | 3 weeks |
| 2nd | 1 month | 2 months | 8 weeks |
| 3rd | 2 months | 2 months | 9 weeks |

In French-speaking Switzerland, courts generally apply the Bernese scale (see the City of Lausanne page).

## When does IJM replace the employer’s obligation?

An IJM policy replaces the legal obligation if a written agreement, a standard contract or a CCT (collective labour agreement) provides for it and its benefits are at least equivalent (art. 324a para. 4 CO).

A CCT can also make IJM compulsory. The national CCT for hotels and restaurants does so in its art. 23 “Daily sickness benefits insurance”. SECO publishes the list of extended CCTs.

## What are the IJM figures for 2027?

No new legal figure applies: the rate and duration of IJM are set by the contract or the CCT. The national CCT for hotels and restaurants, for example, requires 80% of gross salary for 720 days over 900 consecutive days. During the waiting period, of at most 60 days a year, the employer pays 88% of gross salary, and premiums are shared equally (art. 23 CCT).

## What can you compare?

Contracts differ first on the benefit rate, as a percentage of salary, on the duration of benefits and on the waiting period, during which the employer pays itself. The legal framework matters too, since IJM can fall under the LCA or under the LAMal (compulsory basic health insurance). Offers also differ on the transfer to individual insurance when an employee leaves, on the premium, calculated as a percentage of payroll, and on the profit share and its refund clause.

## What are the common pitfalls?

Problems usually come from the waiting period, the equivalence of benefits, an overlooked CCT or termination by the insurer.

1. With a long waiting period, the employer remains exposed for that whole time (art. 324a CO).
2. IJM that is less favourable than the applicable scale does not release the employer from the obligation.
3. An extended CCT can impose IJM even if the employment contract does not mention it.
4. In collective LCA IJM, the insurer can also terminate, in the ordinary way or after a claim (art. 35a para. 4 LCA). The employer may then find itself without cover after a serious case.

## How do you terminate an IJM policy?

Ordinary termination takes effect at the end of the 3rd year, then at the end of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA). After a claim, art. 42 LCA applies, to the insurer as to the policyholder in collective IJM.

## What this page does not cover

This page calculates no premium and does not detail the scales beyond the 3rd year; the [Zurich courts](https://www.gerichte-zh.ch/themen/arbeit/waehrend-arbeitsverhaeltnis/arbeitsverhinderung/krankheit-und-unfall) publish all three scales up to the 25th year of service. It does not deal with optional IJM under the LAMal (art. 67 ff. LAMal). The amendment of the CO planned for 01.07.2027 concerns birth-related leave and does not change art. 324a and 324b.
