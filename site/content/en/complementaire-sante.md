Supplementary health insurance is an optional private-law contract governed by the LCA (Insurance Contract Act). It can be cancelled for the end of the 3rd year of the contract, then for the end of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a para. 1 LCA). It pays for costs that basic insurance does not cover, such as a semi-private or private hospital ward, complementary medicine, glasses, dental care or treatment abroad. Its rules differ greatly from those of the LAMal (compulsory basic health insurance).

## Is it compulsory?

No. Supplementary insurance is a private-law contract governed by the Insurance Contract Act (LCA). The insurer can refuse a person or add reservations. Premiums are not regulated; they depend on age and often on health. There is therefore no official figure for 2027.

## What can you compare?

- Benefits, such as a hospital stay in the general, semi-private or private ward; complementary medicine; glasses; dental care; cover abroad.
- Annual ceilings by type of benefit.
- Deductibles specific to the contract, separate from the LAMal deductible.
- Age brackets, which make the premium rise at certain ages, something the LAMal does not have.
- The minimum term of the contract.
- Exclusions and reservations written in the policy.

Public data is thin: the FSO (OFS) publishes an index of supplementary insurance premiums by canton on opendata.swiss, with no detail by insurer.

## What are the common pitfalls?

1. If you answer a question in the health questionnaire inaccurately or incompletely, that is called non-disclosure. The insurer can then cancel in writing within **4 weeks** of learning of it. It can also refuse benefits, or even ask for them to be repaid, for claims linked to the undisclosed fact (art. 6 LCA).
2. Cancelling the old supplementary insurance before the new insurer has accepted you in writing can leave you without cover if the new insurer refuses.
3. A contract signed for 5 years does not necessarily bind you for 5 years, as a multi-year contract can still be cancelled at the end of the 3rd year (art. 35a para. 1 LCA).
4. Age brackets are easy to overlook, and a premium that is reasonable today can rise sharply at the next one.

## How and when can you cancel?

Ordinary cancellation applies for the end of the **3rd year** or of each following year, with **3 months’** notice, even if the contract was concluded for a longer term (art. 35a para. 1 LCA). Earlier cancellation can be agreed, with the same deadlines for both parties (art. 35a para. 2 LCA). For a contract that follows the calendar year and can be cancelled at the end of 2027, for example, the letter must be received by 30 September 2027 at the latest.

There is also a right of withdrawal: for **14 days**, you can go back on your application or your acceptance (art. 2a LCA).

For supplementary health insurance, only the policyholder can cancel in the ordinary way or after a claim; the insurer has no such right (art. 35a para. 4 LCA). After a partial claim, cancellation remains possible until the indemnity is paid at the latest (art. 42 para. 1 LCA), and only the insured person can invoke this. Nor can the insurer cancel supplementary insurance on the sole ground that you change basic insurer (art. 7 para. 8 LAMal).

## Can you keep supplementary insurance if you change basic insurer?

Yes. Basic and supplementary insurance are two separate contracts, so you can keep your supplementary insurance with one insurer and take basic insurance with another. The deadlines differ: for basic insurance, the letter must be received by 30 November 2026 at the latest for a change on 1 January 2027 (art. 7 para. 2 LAMal). ## What this page does not cover

This page does not compare supplementary insurance premiums, for lack of public data by insurer, and does not replace your general terms, which may be more favourable than the law. The reference text is the LCA, on [Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/24/719_735_717/fr).
