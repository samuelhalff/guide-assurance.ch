Business liability insurance (RC entreprise) covers bodily injury and property damage your business causes to third parties. Professional liability insurance (RC professionnelle) covers pure financial loss, for example a client’s financial loss after wrong advice. They are two separate contracts, and professional liability insurance is compulsory only for some regulated professions, such as lawyers (at least CHF 1 million a year, art. 12 LLCA, Federal Act on the Free Movement of Lawyers).

## Is professional liability insurance compulsory?

In general, no. Some regulated professions must have it, though:

- Lawyers need cover suited to the risks, of at least CHF 1 million a year, which equivalent security can replace (art. 12 let. f LLCA, Federal Act on the Free Movement of Lawyers).
- Doctors practising on their own responsibility must have cover suited to the risks, with no minimum amount in the law (art. 40 let. h LPMéd, Medical Professions Act).
- Non-tied insurance intermediaries can only be registered with professional liability insurance or equivalent financial guarantees (art. 41 para. 2 let. d LSA, Insurance Supervision Act). The minimum is CHF 2 million a year, more if the intermediary employs other intermediaries (art. 189 OS, Supervision Ordinance).

For other regulated professions, the law governing the activity prevails. No general figure applies in 2027.

## What types of damage can liability insurance cover?

The law distinguishes three types of damage:

- bodily injury, when a person is hurt;
- property damage, when a thing is damaged or destroyed;
- pure financial loss, when someone loses money with no bodily injury or property damage, for example after wrong advice.

Business liability insurance covers the first two. Pure financial losses fall under professional liability insurance. A third type of cover, product liability insurance (RC produits), pays for damage caused by a product you make or sell.

## What can you compare?

Contracts differ on the sum insured, per claim and per year, and on the types of damage covered. Product liability may or may not be included. Two clauses deal with timing: retroactive cover applies to events that occurred before the contract started, and extended reporting cover to claims received after it ends. The excess per claim and cover abroad, in particular in the United States, also vary from one offer to another.

## What are the common pitfalls?

Most concern the scope of cover and the date of claims.

1. The business relies on business liability insurance alone, which does not cover pure financial losses caused by wrong advice. Those fall under professional liability insurance.
2. The claims made principle is overlooked. Claims are often covered according to the date of the claim, so if you stop trading without extended reporting cover, a late claim is no longer covered.
3. The sum insured is too low compared with what your client contracts require. Their insurance clauses are worth rereading before you set the amount.

## How do you terminate a liability policy?

Ordinary termination takes effect at the end of the 3rd year, then at the end of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA). After a claim, both the insurer and the policyholder may terminate (art. 42 LCA); with a claims made contract, it is worth knowing what happens to cover for future claims before changing insurer. The right of revocation lasts 14 days from the proposal or its acceptance (art. 2a LCA).

Your premises, machinery and stock fall under [Property insurance](/en/business/property-insurance/), and IT risks under [Business cyber insurance](/en/business/cyber-insurance/).
