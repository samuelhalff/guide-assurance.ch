Cyber insurance for SMEs is optional in Switzerland: it is a private-law contract governed by the LCA (Insurance Contract Act). It pays part of the costs and losses after a cyberattack, provided the security measures declared to the insurer, such as MFA and backups, are actually in place.

After an attack, an SME can be blocked for several days while it recovers encrypted data, restarts its systems and notifies its clients.

## Is cyber insurance compulsory?

No. No law requires an SME to insure against IT risks, and there is no official premium figure. For the frequency of incidents in Switzerland, the Federal Office of Cybersecurity (OFCS) publishes half-yearly reports and reporting statistics.

## What can you compare?

Contracts differ first on the own costs they pay: forensic analysis, restoring systems, crisis management and notifying the persons concerned under the Swiss Data Protection Act (nFADP, nLPD). For business interruption after an attack, the question is which loss is covered and for how long. Data-related liability (RC) answers third-party claims after a leak. Only some contracts insure ransom, and cover for CEO fraud and social engineering varies, as does its cap. The insurer also sets security requirements before signing, for example multi-factor authentication (MFA) and backups. A hotline available 24/7 is another point to compare.

## What are the common pitfalls?

They mostly come from the questionnaire and the exclusions.

1. The questionnaire lists security measures that are not applied. The insurer may then invoke non-disclosure (art. 6 LCA), terminate within 4 weeks and refuse the related benefits.
2. The business assumes CEO fraud is covered, when it is often excluded or capped low.
3. The business counts on the insurance to pay fines, which are generally not insurable.

## How do you terminate a cyber policy?

Ordinary termination takes effect at the end of the 3rd year, then at the end of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA). After a claim, art. 42 LCA applies. The right of revocation lasts 14 days from the proposal or its acceptance (art. 2a LCA).

## What should you prepare before asking for a quote?

The insurer will ask about the state of your backups and whether you use MFA. The questionnaire deserves accurate answers, because the insurer will rely on it after a loss.

Material damage to your premises and machinery falls under [Property insurance](/en/business/property-insurance/). The page [Personal cyber insurance](/en/personal-cyber-insurance/) is for individuals.

## Is there public data on cyber insurance premiums?

No official data gives cyber insurance premiums. The OFCS publishes incident reporting statistics, which show how often incidents occur in Switzerland.

This page does not compare any premium and does not deal with the reporting obligations specific to critical infrastructure. It gives no technical security advice.
