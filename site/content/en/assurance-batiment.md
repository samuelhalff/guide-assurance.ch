In French-speaking Switzerland, building insurance is compulsory in Vaud, Fribourg, Neuchâtel, Jura and the Bernese Jura. A cantonal institution with a monopoly insures every building there against fire and natural hazards. Geneva and Valais have no cantonal institution, and owners choose a private insurer.

## Is building insurance compulsory in each French-speaking canton?

It is compulsory in five of the seven areas below. Each row was checked against an official source on 22.11.2026.

| Canton | Cantonal institution (monopoly) | Obligation to insure the building | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vaud | Yes, ECA Vaud | Yes, buildings **and contents** (art. 6, 6a and 15 LAIEN) | State of Vaud |
| Fribourg | Yes, ECAB | Yes (art. 77 LECAB); buildings below the threshold set by the ECAB, CHF 10,000, are excluded (art. 86 of regulation RSF 732.1.11) | State of Fribourg, ECAB |
| Neuchâtel | Yes, ECAP | Yes (art. 12 and 15 LAB); exceptions for low-value, temporary or mobile buildings | Canton of Neuchâtel |
| Jura | Yes, ECA Jura | Yes, fire and natural hazards (art. 16); exceptions for low-value, temporary or mobile buildings (art. 17) | Jura law RSJU 873.11 |
| Bernese Jura | Yes, Bern property insurance (Assurance immobilière Berne) | Yes, fire and natural hazards (art. 8 of Bern law RSB 873.11) | Canton of Bern |
| Geneva | No | No obligation; the bank requires it in practice for a mortgage | State of Geneva |
| Valais | No | No obligation found in the cantonal law; private market | Valais Council of State |

For Geneva, the State says there is neither a monopoly nor an obligation to insure. For Valais, the Council of State lists the canton among those without public-law property insurance, and we found no obligation in the cantonal law.

## What does the cantonal institution cover, and what does it leave out?

In monopoly cantons, the cantonal institution covers the building against fire and natural hazards. Water damage is not included and needs a private contract. Earthquake is generally not covered automatically.

## What can you compare?

In monopoly cantons (Vaud, Fribourg, Neuchâtel), the comparison is limited to the private top-ups: water damage, glass breakage, building owner’s liability (RC) and earthquake.

In Geneva, the whole contract is open to comparison, including replacement value or current value, natural hazards cover and the excess.

Each cantonal institution sets its own premiums, and we found no 2027 figure.

## What are the common mistakes?

1. The ECA does not cover water damage, which still has to be insured privately.
2. Earthquake is easy to overlook, because it is generally not covered automatically.
3. In a condominium (PPE), the owners’ association often takes out the insurance for the building. It helps to know what each contract covers, yours and the PPE’s.
4. If the building’s valuation is not updated after a renovation, the payout may be reduced for underinsurance.

## Can you cancel building insurance?

In monopoly cantons, the contract with the cantonal institution cannot be cancelled. Private contracts, whether top-ups or a full contract in Geneva and Valais, can be cancelled for the end of the 3rd year, then of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA), or after a claim under art. 42 LCA.

For tenants, contents fall under [household insurance](/en/household-and-liability-insurance/). Building insurance covers the building itself.

## What this page does not cover

This page gives no premiums and does not deal with cantons outside French-speaking Switzerland. Cantonal institutions publish their terms, and their association, the AECA, publishes annual reports on [vkg.ch](https://www.vkg.ch). For your canton, the authoritative source is the cantonal law and the institution concerned.
