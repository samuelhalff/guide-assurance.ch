From 1 January 2028, cantons and health insurers will fund outpatient and inpatient care in basic insurance using a single cost split, with at least 26.9% paid by the cantons and at most 73.1% by premiums. This is the uniform financing of benefits, known by its German acronym EFAS, which voters approved on 24 November 2024. Deductibles and co-payments stay the same.

## How is care funded today?

Today the split depends on where you are treated. For a hospital stay that includes a night, cantons pay at least 55% of the cost and premiums at most 45%. Outpatient treatment, with no overnight stay, is paid entirely by premiums, and the canton pays nothing towards it.

Under this rule, a treatment that moves from hospital to outpatient care drops out of the cantonal share and falls entirely on premiums. EFAS removes that difference between the two kinds of care.

## What does EFAS change from 2028?

From 2028, one split will apply to all outpatient and inpatient benefits. Cantons will pay insurers a contribution of at least 26.9% of net costs, meaning costs after deducting what patients pay themselves. The rest, at most 73.1%, will be funded by premiums.

Cantons will therefore contribute to outpatient treatment for the first time. According to the FOPH, they will pay their contribution to insurers, and it will cover all of these benefits.

## What about nursing homes and home care?

They will follow the same split from 1 January 2032, and their funding stays as it is until then. According to the FOPH, when the switch happens the Federal Council must set patients' contribution to care so that it is no higher than before, and may not raise it for at least four years.

## What changes for policyholders?

According to the FOPH, little will change day to day. Bills from doctors, hospitals and other providers will still go to the insurer, which reimburses them after deducting your cost share. That share is still your chosen deductible plus a 10% co-payment, capped at CHF 700 a year for an adult (art. 64 LAMal and art. 103 para. 2 KVV).

Supplementary insurance is affected on one specific point. For a stay in a hospital that is on no cantonal hospital list, basic insurance will keep paying 45%, while its share rises to 73.1% in listed hospitals. The rest will have to be paid by the patient or a supplementary policy. The general terms of each supplementary policy state whether it covers that balance.

## Will premiums go down?

No official figure answers that question today. In its press release of 1 April 2026, the Federal Council says that cantonal funding of outpatient care should ease the burden on premium payers somewhat. It does not quantify the effect.

EFAS does not change how premiums are set. Each year insurers calculate premiums from expected costs, and the FOPH approves them. For 2027, the FOPH expects costs to rise by 5.0%, and the average adult premium goes up by 4.9% across Switzerland. Our barometer tracks these figures canton by canton.

## Where does implementation stand?

The law has been passed, but the implementing ordinances have not yet been adopted. The Federal Council put the amendments out for consultation from 1 April to 8 July 2026. The FOPH is now reviewing the responses and preparing changes. The amended ordinances are due to take effect on 1 January 2028.

Several details will depend on those ordinances, such as the exact cantonal shares between 2028 and the inclusion of long-term care in 2032. We will add them to this article once the Federal Council adopts them.
