No federal law requires household insurance or personal liability insurance (RC privée). Vaud, Fribourg and Jura do, however, require you to insure your contents against fire, and landlords often ask for liability cover when you sign a lease (ch.ch). The two contracts are often sold together.

## What does household insurance cover, and what does liability insurance cover?

Household insurance protects your own belongings against fire, natural hazards, theft and water damage. Glass breakage is often offered as an option.

Personal liability insurance pays for damage you cause to third parties. In general, it covers everyone in the household (ch.ch).

## Is household or liability insurance compulsory in Switzerland?

No federal law requires either one. Three French-speaking cantons do require your contents to be insured.

- In Vaud, every person or business domiciled in the canton insures their contents against fire and natural hazards with the ECA, the cantonal building insurance institution (art. 6a and 15 LAIEN). A new arrival already insured with a licensed Swiss insurer can keep their contract until it expires, at most 5 years after their belongings arrive (State of Vaud).
- In Fribourg, contents must be insured against fire and forces of nature (art. 1 of law RSF 732.2.1), with a private insurer authorised by the Confederation (art. 4). The head of the household insures the belongings of the whole household (art. 3).
- In Jura, contents must be insured against fire with a private insurer. The law allows two months to take out the contract and prove it to the commune (law RSJU 873.21, art. 4 and 7).

For Geneva, Valais, Neuchâtel and the Bernese Jura, we found no obligation in the cantonal law.

## What sets two contracts apart?

The difference with the biggest consequences is the sum insured for contents. If it is lower than the real value of your belongings, the payout is reduced (underinsurance). Contracts also vary on:

- simple theft away from home, often an option;
- the excess per claim;
- the liability sum, expressed in millions;
- tenant’s liability, for damage to the home you rent;
- occasional driving of other people’s vehicles, covered or not by the liability insurance.

## What are the common mistakes?

1. The value of your contents rises over the years, and a sum insured left unchanged ends up as underinsurance.
2. In Vaud, the ECA already insures contents against fire and natural hazards. A private contract that covers those risks again makes you pay twice; what matters is what it adds.
3. A bike or bag stolen outdoors is often covered only with the theft-away-from-home option.
4. Damage to a rented home is not necessarily all covered, and normal wear and tear is excluded.

## When can you cancel household insurance?

The contract can be cancelled for the end of the 3rd year, then of each year, with 3 months’ notice (art. 35a LCA). After a claim, both the insurer and the policyholder can end it (art. 42 LCA), and a 14-day right of withdrawal runs from the application or acceptance (art. 2a LCA). In Vaud, the ECA contract follows your domicile and cannot be cancelled like a private contract.
